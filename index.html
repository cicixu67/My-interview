Same School but different major

Two Parsons students with Two different design majors.

This project compares my experience as a Communication Design student with my friend's Icey's experience as a Strategic Design and Management student under the same school.

My Experience

Communication Design

As a Communication Design student, a lot of my work focuses on visual communication, making things, and thinking about how an idea can be shown to other people.

Classes

My classes involve design projects, visual experiments, research, and critiques.

Projects

I work on different kinds of visual projects and usually have to develop an idea from the beginning to the final design.

Workload

A lot of the work happens outside of class because projects take time to develop and revise.

Icey's Experience

Strategic Design and Management

Icey is a Parsons student studying Strategic Design and Management. Her experience is different from mine because her major looks at design from a more strategic and business-related perspective.

Classes

There is a lot of research, teamwork, discussion, and presentations. Icey expected there to be more business-related classes before starting Parsons.

Projects

A lot of the projects involve research and trying to understand a problem. Students might collect information, talk to people, work with a group, and then develop a solution or strategy.

Workload

The workload includes project work, readings, research, group meetings, and presentations. Sometimes one project takes a long time because the idea keeps developing and changing.

The Interview

I interviewed Icey to learn more about her experience at Parsons and compare it with my own experience.

1. Why did you choose Strategic Design and Management at Parsons?

I chose Strategic Design and Management because I was interested in design, but I didn't want to focus only on making visual work. I was also interested in business, strategy, and understanding how organizations work. I felt like SDM could combine those different interests.

2. Before you started at Parsons, what did you think your major would be like?

I thought there would be more business-related classes. But after I started, I realized there was actually a lot of research, teamwork, discussion, and presentations. It was a little different from what I expected.

3. What does a typical week look like for you as an SDM student?

It depends on the semester, but usually I have a lot of project work, readings, research, group meetings, and presentations. Sometimes one project takes a long time because we keep developing and changing the idea.

4. How is your major different from Communication Design?

I think Communication Design focuses more on how an idea is communicated visually, while SDM focuses more on the idea itself, the strategy behind it, and how it works in a larger context.

5. Do you spend more time researching or actually making things?

I think we spend a lot of time researching and thinking before making something. We definitely make things, but the final product isn't always the most important part. Sometimes it's more about testing an idea or communicating a strategy.

6. What kinds of projects do you usually work on?

A lot of our projects involve research and trying to understand a problem. We might collect information, talk to people, work with a group, and then develop a solution or strategy.

7. Do you have a lot of group projects?

Yeah, I think group work is pretty common. It's nice because everyone has different ideas and experiences, but sometimes it's difficult because everyone has different schedules and different ways of working.

8. Do you think your workload is different from mine as a Communication Design student?

I think it's different rather than necessarily easier or harder. Communication Design students might spend more time making and revising visual work, while we might spend more time researching, discussing, writing, and preparing presentations.

9. What are critiques like in your major?

We get feedback, but it often focuses on the reasoning behind the project. People might ask about our research, why we chose a certain direction, or whether our strategy makes sense.

10. What skills do you think you're developing through SDM?

I think I'm developing research, communication, presentation, and teamwork skills. I'm also learning how to explain not only what my idea is, but why the idea is important.

11. What has surprised you most about your major?

I think I was surprised by how much communication there is. I expected to do more design-making, but there is actually a lot of talking, presenting, and explaining ideas.

12. What is your favorite part of being an SDM student?

I like being able to look at a problem from different perspectives. I also like that the projects can connect design with real-world problems instead of only focusing on the visual result.

13. Do you think studying SDM has changed the way you look at everyday life?

Yeah, I think so. I pay more attention to why people behave in certain ways and why systems work the way they do. Sometimes I notice myself thinking about those things without really realizing it.

14. What do you think Communication Design students might misunderstand about SDM?

Maybe people think SDM is mostly business, but I don't think that's true. There is still a lot of design thinking. It's just that we look at design from a broader perspective.

15. What do you think SDM students might misunderstand about Communication Design?

I think people might assume Communication Design is mostly about making things look good. But there is actually a lot of thinking, concept development, and communication behind visual design.

16. What do you want to do after graduation?

I'm still figuring it out, but I want to do something that combines design and strategy. I like working on problems, understanding different perspectives, and developing ideas that could actually be useful.

17. If you could try Communication Design for one semester, would you?

Yeah, I think it would be interesting. I would like to learn more about visual communication and experience the making process from a different perspective.

18. If you had to describe the biggest difference between our two student experiences in one sentence, what would you say?

I would say Communication Design focuses more on how an idea is communicated visually, while Strategic Design and Management focuses more on how an idea works within a larger system or context.

What Do We Have in Common?

Even though our majors are different, we are both design students at Parsons. We both develop ideas, work through projects, and think about how design can solve problems.

What Is Different?

Communication Design focuses more on how an idea is communicated visually, while Strategic Design and Management focuses more on the idea, the strategy behind it, and how it works in a larger context.

What I Noticed

Before doing this interview, I thought our majors might be more similar than they actually are. Talking to Icey made me realize that even though we are both studying design at Parsons, we can approach design in very different ways.

My experience is more focused on visual communication and making, while Icey's experience involves more research, strategy, discussion, and presentations. At the same time, both majors involve developing ideas and thinking about how design can be useful.
